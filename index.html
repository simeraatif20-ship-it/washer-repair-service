Washer Leaking Water? 7 Causes and How to Fix Each

Updated November 10, 2026 · 6 minute read · Top-load and front-load washers

Quick answer: where the water shows up tells you what failed. Water at the front door of a front-loader is almost always the door gasket. Water at the back is a hose or inlet valve. Water under the middle of the machine is the drain pump, a drain hose, or the tub seal. Shut off the supply valves and unplug the washer before you look.

Most washer leaks start small and get blamed on the wrong part. Dry the floor, put a few dry paper towels or a sheet of cardboard under the machine, run a short cycle, and note where the first wet spot appears. 1. Worn or dirty door gasket

Part $40–$120 · Labor $100–$200 · 30–60 minutes

The rubber ring around a front-loader door seals the drum to the cabinet. It cracks, tears on a zipper or underwire, and collects hair and lint that hold the seal open. Mold in the lower fold hides small cuts.

Check it: pull the rubber lip back and wipe the whole ring, especially the lowest fold. Look for a slit or a thin, gray area. A dry towel pressed around the ring during a short cycle shows the exact spot.

Leave the door cracked open after every load. Trapped moisture is what rots a gasket in two or three years instead of ten.

2. Loose or split fill hoses

Hose set $10–$30 · Labor $0–$80 · 15 minutes

The two hoses between the wall valves and the back of the washer carry full line pressure. Rubber hoses bulge, crack and weep at the fittings. A leak that only appears while the washer fills points here.

Check it: wipe each hose dry, turn the water on, and run a finger along the length and around each coupling. Tighten fittings a quarter turn by hand or with pliers, and replace any hose with a bulge or a crusty white ring at the end.

3. Cracked or loose drain hose

Part $10–$25 · 15–30 minutes

The drain hose is the corrugated one that loops up to the standpipe. A clamp that has loosened, a kink, or a hose pushed too far down the standpipe can send water onto the floor during drain and spin.

Check it: confirm the hose is clamped at the machine end, the loop sits high enough, and the standpipe isn't backed up. If the standpipe overflows, the leak is a household drain clog, not the washer.

4. Drain pump or pump filter leak

Part $35–$80 · Labor $100–$180 · 30–60 minutes

On a front-loader the pump filter sits behind a small panel at the bottom front. A cap that isn't seated, a cracked filter housing or a pump with a worn seal drips during drain.

Check it: open the panel, drain the trap into a shallow pan, clean the filter, and screw it back in all the way until it stops. If water still seeps from the pump body or a hose clamp, the pump needs replacing.

5. Detergent drawer overflow

Too much detergent, standard detergent in an HE machine, or a drawer housing packed with residue pushes suds and water out the front. Rinse the drawer and its slot with hot water, use the amount on the HE detergent label, and check that the washer isn't filling too slowly from low water pressure.

6. Unlevel machine or an overloaded drum

A washer that rocks during spin throws water over the tub lip or against the door seal. Level all four feet with a bubble level and tighten the lock nuts. Wash bulky items like comforters alone and leave a hand's width of space at the top of the drum.

7. Failed tub seal or bearing

Part $50–$200 · Labor $250–$450 · Tech job

Water dripping from the center underneath, often with a grinding or rumbling noise during spin, points to the tub seal and bearings behind the drum. The fix means pulling the drum assembly apart, which is why it costs far more than any other leak repair.

On a washer older than about 8 years, a bearing and seal job often lands near half the price of a new machine. Get a written estimate before deciding.

Stop the damage while you diagnose

  • Close both supply valves behind the washer and unplug it.
  • Mop and fan the floor dry within a day so the subfloor doesn't swell.
  • Set a shallow tray or a water alarm under the machine until it is fixed.
  • If a puddle reaches the base of the cabinet or the outlet, keep the washer unplugged and call a technician.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my washer leaking from the bottom?

Water at the bottom usually comes from a loose or split hose, a leaking drain pump, or a worn tub seal. Slide the washer out, dry the floor, and run a short cycle while watching where the first drop appears.

Why does my front-load washer leak from the door?

A torn, moldy or dirty door gasket is the usual cause. Check the lower fold of the rubber for a cut, and wipe out trapped hair and lint, which can break the seal.

Can I run a washer that is leaking?

Not for long. Water on the floor can damage subfloor and cabinets, and water near the electronics can short the board. Shut off the supply valves and unplug the machine until you find the source.

How often should I replace washer hoses?

Rubber fill hoses are commonly replaced every 5 years. Braided stainless hoses last longer, but still check the connections twice a year.

Is a leaking washer worth fixing?

Hoses, gaskets and pumps are cheap fixes worth doing. A leaking tub seal or cracked tub on an older machine often costs more than half the price of a new washer.

Why does water leak from the detergent drawer?

Too much detergent, a clogged drawer housing or low water pressure can push suds and water out of the drawer. Use HE detergent in the right amount and rinse the drawer and its housing.
